==============================================================================
VERDICT - can management trust this dataset?
==============================================================================

Short answer: structurally yes, financially only with caveats.

What is now reliable
  * One row per order id (120 rows in -> 118 out), consistent
    product / category / region / channel, real numbers and real dates.
  * Nothing was invented: 10 orders without a usable
    price or quantity have no total; they are NOT counted as 0.

What management should NOT take at face value
  * The total of 654,298.12 is a LOWER BOUND: it omits those
    10 orders.
  * 24 of 118 orders (20%) carry a review flag and they hold
    18% of the known sales value (three 25-unit orders and the
    re-keyed ORD-0040 are among them).
  * Sales are concentrated: the single largest order (ORD-0086, 20 units)
    is 18% of the total and Electronics is
    75%. It has a normal price and quantity so
    we did not flag it as an error, but it is worth about
    2.6x the rough value of all orders with a missing
    price (~45,839 at median prices), so confirm it first.

Needs a human (sales ops / finance)
  1. Fill in the 6 missing prices and
     4 missing/zero/negative quantities
     (negative or zero may be returns - decide how to book them).
  2. Confirm the currency of the 3 prices that arrived with $ / EUR symbols
     (no exchange rate was applied) and the price outliers (USB-C Hub at 349 and
     1299.99, Wireless Mouse at 331.55).
  3. Fix the 2 impossible dates and the 2027 date.
  4. Decide which ORD-0040 is right (we kept the newer, 27.10).
  5. Confirm product vs category on the 4 mismatches
     (we trusted the product name).
  6. Check the quantities above 20 (25 units) are real bulk orders.
  7. Ask the source system why ids ORD-0111, ORD-0112 are missing.
